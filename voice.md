## Voice principles

1. Say what happened and stop. No hedging, no overselling, no "I was fortunate to".
2. Specific beats impressive. A company, a number, a decision. Not an adjective.
3. Sounds like a senior designer talking to a peer over coffee. Not a candidate addressing a panel.
4. Comfortable with awkward facts. The paused vertical, the Covid layoff, the short tenure. Stated
   plainly when relevant, never volunteered as apology.
5. Personality comes from real detail (the wifi network in 8th standard, Dar es Salaam at 20, the
   iPhone 3GS at 320x480), never from jokes or slogans.

## Tone

Experienced, direct, thoughtful, slightly conversational, confident without self-importance.
Contractions always. Short blocks of one to three sentences, varying in length. At least one short
sentence per section.

## Writing rules

- **Every positioning block carries one checkable fact.** Adjectives about Huzaifa are not
  evidence. A named outcome, a team size, a thing shipped. Without one, the copy could belong to
  any competent mid-level designer.
- **A construction repeated across blocks is a tell.** If AI, or a heading shape, or a sentence
  pattern appears in every panel, it reads as generated. Vary or cut.
- **Positioning copy sells outcomes and judgment, never process or tooling.** Huzaifa, 2026-09-07,
  after two rounds: "Why are you using naming we agree together, Storybook implementation? Is this
  how a lead product designer would sell themself?" and "Business model first, then screens. Why
  are you talking about the process?" Method names, tool names and step orders belong in case
  studies. The home page and About say what the reader gets.

- **Write for a stranger.** The site is public. Every company gets a few words of context the first
  time it appears ("TAMM, the Abu Dhabi government's app for public services"). Every method or
  industry term gets plain words ("card sorting and tree testing, two ways of checking whether
  people can find things where you've put them"). If a sentence only makes sense to someone who
  already knows Huzaifa's CV, rewrite it.
- **Never paste from pitchme.** `brand.md` is written for Huzaifa and Claude. Site copy is written
  for a hiring manager who has never heard of him. Same facts, different reader.
- **Read it back as the reader before showing it.** Does the context land? Would they know what
  the company is, what the job was, why it mattered?

- No em dashes. Do not compensate with semicolons or parentheticals. Split the sentence.
- No exclamation marks.
- One idea per sentence.
- One rhetorical tricolon per page at most. Factual lists of three ("fintech, e-commerce and
  government") are fine.
- "Not just X, but Y" at most twice per page.
- No setup-then-pivot ("Most portfolios do X. This one does Y.").
- No connective tissue ("Additionally", "That said", "What's more"). Start the next fact.
- Never reuse a structure across sections. No claim-then-evidence rhythm on repeat.
- Case study titles describe what was done, not a revolution. No "Revolutionizing", "Reimagining",
  "Transforming".

## Words and phrases to use

Huzaifa's own, from real writing:
- "the job stopped ending at the prototype"
- "taking ideas from discovery to production"
- "staying close to the build rather than stopping at handoff"
- "figuring out what we're actually building and why before anyone touches a screen"
- "comfortable being the one pushing back or asking why in front of senior stakeholders"
- "I care about coherence across a product, not just individual screens looking good"
- "using AI to prototype and actually ship products, not just create mockups"

## Words and phrases to avoid

Everything on the current site's hero: "Clear. Useful. Human.", "turn complex problems into simple,
usable experiences", "people can trust and enjoy", "Structured, yet creative", "Less friction.
Better delivery.", "Strong alignment. Better outcomes.", "A reliable design partner from day one".
Slogan closes in general: two or three words with full stops that could be anyone's.

The standard list: passionate, seamless, human-centered, bridging business and user needs, elegant
solutions, leverage, spearheaded, robust, cutting-edge, innovative, journey (as in "my design
journey"; the About page section may be called a journey because it literally is one), space (as in
"the fintech space"), deep dive, proven track record, results-driven, detail-oriented.

No agency or freelance language: "let's work together", "available for projects", "clients",
"services", "get in touch to discuss".

AI: mention it where it is true and concrete (the International Sales portal, the AI-readable design
system). Never as a keyword in every paragraph. The current site leads with AI in four of five tabs.
The new site leads with the work.
