Building one home for Abu Dhabi’s government services

TAMM is Abu Dhabi’s initiative to bring government services into one ecosystem. The services were fragmented, so we built one unified digital system where citizens, residents, businesses and visitors complete them in one place.

Focus

The challenge

Thousands of government services, in one app, for millions of people.

TAMM is where citizens, residents, businesses and visitors in Abu Dhabi apply for government services, talk to customer service and track their requests. Before, each service lived with its own department. Bringing them into one app risked too much information, and journeys that broke between departments.

Heuristic evaluation

First, we reviewed the app as it was.

As a team, we went through the first version of the app screen by screen, against usability heuristics.

Information architecture

From who owns a service, to what people need.

With what the evaluation showed, I led the rework of the app’s structure: from one built around government departments, to one built around people’s needs. Five places, each with a clear job. Home shows what needs attention. Services is where you find one. Support is where you get help and follow a case. Community is news and public holidays. Locker holds everything that is yours.

Testing the structure

Then we went back to users.

We did not guess the new structure was better. Card sorting showed how people group the services, and a tree test checked whether they could find things in it: 8 tasks, 35 people.

88%success
75%directness

UI design

Then we designed it, section by section.

Three of us redesigned the whole app on the new structure. We split it into its sections and designed each one to the same rules, so it looks and works the same everywhere.

Onboarding

TAMM only offered login with UAE PASS. At the time UAE PASS was new: most residents did not know what it was, and did not have a verified account. So onboarding had to teach people how to become a verified UAE PASS user, and then log in. We prototyped different versions of that flow and tested them with residents in the real world, in sessions I moderated.

Reflection

Clarity beats cleverness.

As a senior designer on this project, I learned that clarity beats cleverness. In a government context, users don’t want to be “delighted” by animations. They want to get their tasks done so they can get back to their lives.
